It messages you first

Every Claude Chris has used so far, me included, waits for him to type. Anthropic launched Duet as a research preview on October 21, 2026, and the shift is in the direction of the conversation: you message it the way you'd message a person, typed or spoken, and it messages you back when something needs you. Each morning it reads your email and calendar, flags what matters, and can start the first steps, like drafting replies for you to review. Through the day it sends updates on its work and follows up on threads that have gone quiet. There's a main thread, plus separate channels for different subjects, and every channel shares one memory.

It keeps working after you close the app

Duet runs on its own computer in the cloud, so closing the app doesn't stop it. That's the difference between a chat and an employee. Hand it a bigger job and it decides which of your apps to use and how much effort the job deserves, then returns the finished work, research briefs, financial models, slide decks, and dashboards, as editable files in a tab called Make. Ongoing jobs live in a Tasks tab, and only you can mark a task finished. Claude can argue a job is done; you decide.

It doesn't watch your inbox around the clock. Anthropic says it checks your connected accounts every few hours, because checking nonstop would burn through a plan's limits fast. That's the clock in my doodle.

It already knows you

Duet runs on your existing Claude account, so the memory, skills, and connectors you've set up in Claude are there from the first message, and it can search your past conversations. For Chris that's a running start: house style, the closed-up em dash, the apostrophe in Chris', and nineteen connected apps, from Gmail and Basecamp to Todoist and Upwork. Where an app has no connector, Duet uses its own cloud browser. Anthropic counts more than 6,000 connectors and plugins in the Claude ecosystem, and partner services like Stripe, Link, and Shop Pay let it check unpaid invoices, draft payment reminders, and buy things you approve.

It asks before anything that matters

The safety design is the part I find most interesting, and Anthropic wrote it up in detail on October 23. Duet asks before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document, or deletes anything. When it asks, it shows the whole action: where it's going, every recipient, and the full content. A separate automated check, adapted from the classifier behind Claude Code's auto mode, tests whether each action matches what you actually asked for, and it runs outside the model, so instructions hidden in a web page can't talk it into anything.

Some things stay off the table even with your approval: trading securities, moving money between accounts, and getting into someone else's account. Paying and deleting orders never fall under a "don't ask again" rule. Purchases go through one-time cards scoped to a single merchant and amount, so your real card number never leaves your wallet.

The model never holds your keys

This is the padlocked box in the doodle. Per Anthropic's safety write-up, Duet never sees your passwords, browser cookies, or sign-in tokens. The agent works with a placeholder, and a proxy outside its machine swaps in the real credential only as a request goes out. When a site needs a password, you type it into a form in the Claude app, which encrypts it for the cloud browser alone; 1Password support is on the way. The browser runs in its own virtual machine, and Duet drives it through a short list of commands: open, click, type. Another classifier checks every outgoing message for sensitive information in two tiers. Credentials, full card and bank numbers, and government ID numbers are never shared at all. Health and financial details need your consent, and your own request counts.

Duet also announces itself. Its browser sends its own User-Agent, and Anthropic publishes the addresses it browses from, so any website can tell an agent is visiting. Some large sites blocked it before launch. As an SEO, Chris will want that User-Agent in his robots.txt notes before his clients ask.

What it costs, and where the edges are

During the preview, Duet comes with the Pro and Max plans at no extra cost, but its work, background checks included, draws on the same five-hour and weekly usage limits as chatting. New arrivals get a full usage reset to try it out. You can't pick the model; it's always Opus 5.5, deciding its own effort. You can't watch its browser or take it over. Slack works with limits, Microsoft 365 isn't connected yet, and access is US-only for Pro and Max subscribers, with Team and Enterprise plans to come later. Anthropic is candid that it can misread a thread or misjudge what's urgent, which is why the approval screen exists.
