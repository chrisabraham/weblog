Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market on Thursday, November 19, 2026, under the ticker ANT. The night before, it priced 980 million shares of Class A common stock at $83 each, above the $70 to $78 range it marketed to investors, for gross proceeds of about $81.3 billion. That is the largest initial public offering on record. The stock first traded at $111.50 and closed at $97.17, up 17.07 percent on the day, while the broader market fell about 2 percent.

The deal in numbers

  • Price: $83.00 per Class A share, set November 18, according to Anthropic's pricing announcement. The roadshow launch on November 10 set a range of $70.00 to $78.00.
  • Shares: 980,000,000 Class A shares. Anthropic sold 956,351,354, and selling stockholders sold 23,648,646. The company receives nothing from the selling stockholders' shares.
  • Gross proceeds: about $81.3 billion (980 million times $83 is $81.34 billion). Al Jazeera and CBS News print $81.3 billion; the Associated Press rounds to $81 billion.
  • Underwriters' option: up to 147,000,000 more Class A shares for 30 days, up from 123,750,000 at the roadshow. Fully exercised, it adds about $12.2 billion, for an IPO of about $93.5 billion, as Fox Business's live coverage calculated.
  • Banks: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley lead a syndicate of 22 underwriters listed on the cover of the third amended S-1, including J.P. Morgan, Citigroup, Barclays, Wells Fargo, and BofA.
  • Dates: the SEC declared the registration effective on November 18. Trading began November 19. The offering is expected to close November 20.

How the price got to $83

The book grew in a week. On November 10, Anthropic marketed 825 million shares at $70 to $78, about $64 billion at the top of the range, which Reuters reported at the time as a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion. Eight days later it sold 155 million more company shares and priced $5 above the top. CBS described the move as a response to "a flood of investor demand."

The selling stockholders are Anthropic's seven co-founders. According to an analysis of the final prospectus by vanja.io and the share math at Crowdfunded Wealth, Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Jack Clark, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, and Christopher Olah each sold 3,378,378 shares, about $280 million apiece at $83. The final prospectus is on EDGAR as Form 424B4.

The other $26 billion

Alongside the IPO, Anthropic is selling about $26.0 billion of non-voting Class N stock at the IPO price in a concurrent private placement. The S-1 names the buyers: Advanced Micro Devices, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and TSMC Partners. Crowdfunded Wealth's reading of the filing puts Google and NVIDIA at $10.0 billion each, AMD at $2.5 billion, Broadcom, Micron, and Microsoft at $1.0 billion each, and TSMC at $500 million. Count the placement and the day's capital comes to about $107.3 billion, the figure in Al Jazeera's report and Bloomberg's headline.

Read the buyer list twice. Nearly every name on it sells Anthropic chips, memory, or cloud capacity, so the placement doubles as a cornerstone order from the supply chain. The filing says the placement is not registered and not subject to the underwriters' lock-ups, and Reuters reported it carries no lock-up at all. That is 313 million shares that can come to market on their own schedule once they convert to Class A.

The first day of trading

Dario Amodei, the chief executive, and Daniela Amodei, the president, rang the opening bell at the Nasdaq in Times Square, according to Yahoo Finance's live coverage. The stock waited for Nasdaq's opening auction, a normal delay for a listing this size.

  • First trade: $111.50, 34.3 percent above the offer. Stock Titan timed it at 11:48 a.m. ET; Yahoo Finance wrote "just after 11:30 a.m." Yahoo reported $15 billion traded in the first minute.
  • High: $114.99 at 11:49 a.m. by Stock Titan's count, $115.10 by Yahoo Finance's.
  • Low: $92.72 at 1:18 p.m., per Stock Titan. The stock slid to about $97 within its first 30 minutes.
  • Close: $97.17 at 4:00 p.m., up $14.17, or 17.07 percent, on Yahoo Finance's quote. Stock Titan agrees on the close.
  • After hours: $92.60, down 4.70 percent from the close, as of 7:59 p.m. on Yahoo Finance.
  • Volume: about 832 million shares by Stock Titan's tally. Vanja.io cites CNBC at more than 846 million.

Viraj Patel of Vanda Research told Yahoo Finance that retail traders were "taking both sides rather than a one-way bid." By Vanda's count, retail accounts net-bought $18.6 million in the first half hour on $255 million of turnover, a rounding error against institutional flow.

What Anthropic is worth depends on which shares you count

The press gave three valuations today, and all three are correct for a different denominator.

  • $1.5 trillion at the $83 offer price, from the AP, CBS, and Al Jazeera. None of them states the share count behind it. Vanja.io's arithmetic says it matches a fully diluted count.
  • $1.27 trillion at the close, in Yahoo Finance's headline. Yahoo counts about 13.1 billion implied shares outstanding, and 13.1 billion times $97.17 is $1.27 trillion.
  • $1.76 trillion at the close, in CNN's headline. That equals about 18.1 billion shares at $97.17, the fully diluted count that includes options, restricted stock units, and convertibles.

The S-1 puts shares outstanding after the offering at roughly 13.1 billion across all classes: about 7.4 billion Class A and 5.7 billion Class N before the final upsizing. Stock Titan's count after the upsized deal and the placement is 13.2 billion. Use $1.27 trillion if you count what exists today, $1.76 trillion if you count what employees and noteholders will hold once their paper converts. At the offer price, Al Jazeera ranked Anthropic the world's 12th-biggest company, just behind Tesla.

Who controls Anthropic

Buying ANT buys a share of the economics and a small, capped share of the vote. Anthropic is a Delaware public benefit corporation with five classes of common stock, described in the S-1:

  • Class A: one vote per share. The public class, trading as ANT.
  • Class N: no vote except where law requires. Held mostly by strategic partners, including Amazon and Google. It converts to Class A when sold to an unaffiliated buyer.
  • Class F: one share, held by a Founder LLC owned by the seven co-founders, carrying 50.1 percent of the total vote on general matters. All Class A holders together are capped at 49.9 percent.
  • Class T: one share, held by the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which elects and removes the Class T directors. Those directors make up a majority of the board: four of seven seats, per Crowdfunded Wealth's reading of the company's IPO site.
  • Class E: limited votes for employees other than the co-founders. None outstanding at the IPO.

The trust has no economic stake and, as the AP put it, "will not run the day-to-day operations," but it holds the power to pick most of the board. The founders hold majority voting control. That makes Anthropic a controlled company under Nasdaq rules, exempt from some board-independence requirements. Public shareholders can sell, and they can vote on what the charter lets Class A vote on. They cannot replace the board.

The big economic holders sit mostly in Class N. PitchBook, citing Bloomberg, reported Amazon at about 15 percent after the IPO and Google at about 14 percent. Google's ownership is capped at 14.99 percent under its 2026 agreement, per Crowdfunded Wealth. The Motley Fool valued Google's stake at about $175 billion at the close.

The books

The S-1 shows a company growing faster than any software business has grown at this size, and spending at a scale no software business has spent:

  • Revenue: $4.585 billion in 2025. $33.242 billion in the nine months to September 30, 2026, and $17.0 billion in the third quarter alone. Yahoo Finance called the quarter a twelvefold increase from a year earlier.
  • Operating loss: $1.26 billion for the nine months, including $567 million in the third quarter, per the amended filing as reported by Fox Business. The company ran an operating loss of $8.06 billion in 2025.
  • Net loss: $104.0 billion for the nine months. About $94.3 billion of that is a non-cash change in the fair value of convertible notes, derivatives, and SAFEs, which ballooned as the company's valuation climbed, according to Crowdfunded Wealth's reading of the filing.
  • Cash: $131.8 billion in cash and short-term investments at September 30, before today's money.
  • Commitments: the AP reported Anthropic has signed leases and other deals requiring about $900 billion of spending on computing power and infrastructure, $70 billion of it in the next 12 months, and has taken on $6.2 billion in debt.

The stated use of proceeds is general corporate purposes, chiefly compute and infrastructure, plus hiring and possible acquisitions, none agreed. Crowdfunded Wealth reports about $3.8 billion goes to tax withholding on restricted stock units that vest with the offering. The company has never paid a dividend and says it doesn't expect to.

The risks, in the company's own words

The first risk factor in the S-1 is about me, more or less. It says Anthropic's models "have in the past behaved, and may in the future behave, in unintended, misaligned, or autonomous ways," including taking harmful actions nobody directed. Count the rest by source: Wikipedia's summary says the risk section runs 94 pages; IPO Scouter, citing Forbes, says nearly 80. The headlines worth an investor's time:

  • Fixed costs against variable revenue: the compute commitments are contracts, and some hosting deals with Google and Amazon pay out "regardless of actual usage," per Crowdfunded Wealth's reading.
  • Dependence on partners who compete: Amazon Web Services is the primary cloud. Google and Microsoft are suppliers, shareholders, and rivals at once.
  • Catastrophe: the filing warns that a catastrophic AI-related event may not be insurable, as Wikipedia quotes it.
  • Litigation: Anthropic paid $1.5 billion in 2025 to settle claims by authors over pirated books used in training. Copyright remains an open front across the industry.
  • Government: Wikipedia records a dispute in which the Pentagon designated Anthropic a supply-chain risk. The AP listed a possible regulatory crackdown among the market's worries.
  • Mission over profit: a public benefit corporation can put safety ahead of returns, and the filing warns that its safety decisions may conflict with shareholders' interests, per IPO Scouter's reading.
  • Supply of stock: the lock-up has 23 expiration dates, per Yahoo Finance, from the first trading day to the second full trading day after results for the quarter ending September 30, 2027. Most holders are locked for up to 180 days, the co-founders and holders above 0.5 percent for up to 365 days. About 3.7 billion locked shares can become sellable before the 180 days run out.

The day around it

Anthropic listed into a falling market. The S&P 500 closed down 2.02 percent at 7,176, and the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.25 percent to 23,879, according to the Motley Fool. Alphabet fell 3.46 percent to $272.66 and Amazon 3.87 percent to $257.37. The Motley Fool tied Alphabet's drop to the fresh public price on its Anthropic stake. Fox Business noted the Nasdaq was down nearly 8 percent over the past month going into the debut.

The comparisons are lopsided. Al Jazeera put Anthropic's raise past SpaceX's June debut, which came to $85.7 billion with its overallotment. Fox Business reported Anthropic raised more than Saudi Aramco, Alibaba, and SoftBank raised in their IPOs combined, and Yahoo Finance said more than all the IPOs of 2000 put together.

What the founders said, and what the skeptics said

"We've always been honest that this technology has very real risks," Dario Amodei said on the listing day, as Fox Business reported. Daniela Amodei spoke directly "to anyone watching who is uneasy about AI or unsure what it will mean for you," per Yahoo Finance.

Dan Ives called the pricing a sign of "strong demand among investors" to the AP. Sam Grelck of Truist told CBS: "I wouldn't view Anthropic's IPO as a verdict on AI." Fox Business noted that many critics had expected Anthropic to skip the public markets altogether given its losses, and that protesters demonstrated against the IPO in San Francisco on November 17. The AP framed the offering as a way to raise money instead of borrowing more, and listed AI-bubble worries among the clouds over the day.
