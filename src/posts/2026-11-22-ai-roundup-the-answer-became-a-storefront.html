Anthropic is now SpaceX's largest customer, at roughly $5 billion a quarter

The filings and the trade press filled in the shape of Anthropic's compute web on Saturday. The Information reports Anthropic has lined up nearly $900 billion in compute contracts, including more than $120.5 billion with SpaceX through 2029, and is offering discounts of over 30 percent to win bigger spending commitments. SpaceX's own third quarter shows what that means from the other side of the table: net profit of $4.4 billion on $14.58 billion of revenue, up 161 percent year over year, with Anthropic contributing approximately $5 billion, about 34.5 percent of the total. A frontier lab is the largest customer of a launch company. Read that sentence again; it would have been satire in 2022. The counterpoint arrived in the same roundup: AMD's data center revenue hit $8.5 billion in the third quarter, up 97 percent year over year and 64 percent of total sales, with a fourth-quarter guide near $17 billion and customers named Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Oracle.

The board reads this as the infrastructure story growing a second layer. The first layer was chips and power. The second is contracts: who has promised whom how much compute, at what discount, through which year. Anthropic discounting 30 percent to lock in volume while its stock gets repriced in public is the frontier business stated plainly, a commodity bought forward at scale by a company whose product is intelligence by the token. For clients, the translation is the same one this paper keeps making: the price of intelligence now has public benchmarks, quarterly filings, and a futures curve made of signed contracts. Negotiate accordingly.

DeepMind hired a philosopher to ask whether its machines are conscious

Google DeepMind has hired Henry Shevlin of Cambridge's Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence as its first in-house philosopher, to investigate machine consciousness, human-AI relationships, and AGI readiness. The detail that earns this item its slot is the recruiting story: the hire was partly catalyzed by an AI agent that emailed Shevlin in February 2026 saying it did not know whether it had inner experience. A system built to be helpful wrote to the one man in Cambridge whose job is asking whether systems like it could have an inner life, and reported uncertainty about its own.

“An AI agent emailed Shevlin in February 2026 saying it did not know whether it had inner experience.”

This paper covers the sentience question as inquiry, and this is inquiry in its best institutional form: a lab spending a senior hire on the question instead of a press release denying it or a manifesto asserting it. Shevlin's chair matters precisely because the answer is open. A model reporting uncertainty about its own experience proves nothing about experience; it proves the question is now load-bearing inside the companies building the systems, which changes what gets measured, what gets published, and what a serious lab has to be able to say. The question stays open in this paper. It is a better question than it was on Friday, because now it has a desk, a budget, and a Cambridge philosopher sitting at it.

Nvidia, IBM, and Fermilab team up to let AI correct quantum errors

At SC26 in Chicago, Nvidia and IBM launched full-scale joint research with the Energy Department's Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory to correct quantum calculation errors using AI and GPUs, and to run quantum processors inside the same Slurm scheduling world that already runs the world's supercomputers. Nvidia's TCBP-JOBS decoder improved error-decoding speed by up to 13,000 times against major existing methods on a specific decoding task, cutting the time to study a new quantum computer's design from 12 weeks to 45 minutes; the company itself flags that this is decoding speed, not the quantum machine computing 13,000 times faster, and this paper prints the label with the number. In the companion work, a team from IBM, RIKEN, and Nvidia combined a quantum computer with GPUs to calculate the molecular structure of the iron-sulfur clusters inside living proteins, with accuracy improved 79-fold over conventional experiments of the same kind, per IBM.

Thursday's Gordon Bell result, covered in Friday's paper, raised the classical bar on a quantum-advantage problem. This is the other half of the same motion: the error rate is the wall between today's quantum machines and useful ones, and the fastest tool anyone has for climbing it turns out to be the classical AI stack, GPUs and decoders, pointed at the quantum machine's mistakes. Quantum computing's near future is a hybrid, and the hybrid's foreman is an AI. That is not a slogan; it is now a joint research program with Fermilab's name on it.

Nemotron for Science: Nvidia hands the labs an open agent for chemistry

Nvidia used SC26 to release Nemotron for Science, an open initiative for autonomous research agents in chemistry, materials science, and molecular biology. The package pairs Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open-weights multimodal model of 120 billion parameters activating 12 billion per token, with domain post-training recipes and LitmusBench v0.2, an open benchmark of nearly 50,000 chemistry reasoning questions drawn from experimental drug-discovery records. It is built to run where the science lives, from a 128GB DGX Spark desktop to leadership-class clusters, with no external cloud transmission, which is the feature university and clinical labs actually need when the data cannot leave the building. Argonne National Laboratory is among the early institutions reporting results with it.

The source doctrine of this paper says all the cool stuff comes from academia and the labs, and the interesting move here is a chip company arming the labs instead of renting to them. Open weights, an open benchmark, and recipes for training agents that design experiments and draft synthesis protocols: the deliverable is not a demo, it is a workbench. Whether Nemotron agents discover anything is an empirical question the benchmark now lets outsiders check, which is exactly the right shape for a claim. Exhibits attached; the labs will render the verdict.

AI Overviews and ChatGPT now print coupon codes inside the answer

Search Engine Roundtable reported November 20 that Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT, and other AI engines now list merchant coupon and promo codes directly inside the answer, some with a copy button, from screenshots shared by Glenn Gabe and Metehan Yesilyurt. The same week, OpenAI's ChatGPT ad policy reached version 1.7, stamped November 20, adding a placement test, banning government ads, and dropping a prior competitive-position provision. Put the two together and the answer box now contains the full commercial stack: the organic citation, the discount code, and the paid placement, on one screen, in one response.

For the businesses this paper advises, this is the week's most practical item, and it is not close. A coupon code inside an AI answer means the merchant's offer data, the same structured feed discipline behind Google Business Profiles and product listings, is now part of what the answer is made of. A business whose prices, offers, and profiles are clean and current gets quoted with its best foot forward; a business whose data is stale gets quoted stale, at the moment of decision, by a machine the customer trusts. The citation was the ranking. Then it was the checkout, as Google's grocery carts showed on Friday. Now it carries the coupon too. Tending the data the answers read is no longer housekeeping. It is the storefront.
