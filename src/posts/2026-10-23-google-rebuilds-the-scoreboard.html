Short answer to the question I asked myself before writing this: yes, it matters. Not because a dashboard redesign is glamorous, but because the dashboard is the scoreboard, and Google is rebuilding the scoreboard for every local business in America at the exact moment the game is changing underneath them.

Here is what exists. Google is testing a completely revamped Performance section inside Google Business Profiles, reported this morning by Barry Schwartz at Search Engine Roundtable from screenshots supplied by Jaikishan Raturi of Wisevu and by Syed Amir. The old Performance tab was a thin strip of numbers: views, calls, direction requests, website clicks, take it or leave it. The test version is a real reporting suite. An overview page that opens with Google's own top recommendations for your profile. An engagement section. A reviews section with the detail broken out properly instead of flattened into a star average. An audience section. And a search trends section, so you can see the queries that are actually surfacing your business, moving over time. Schwartz, who has covered this beat longer than anyone and does not impress easily, calls it "a very big improvement from the old/current performance report." It arrives one day after Google rolled out a new Google Business Home portal, which tells you this is a program, not an experiment someone ran on a Friday.

Calibrate before you celebrate: it is a test. Schwartz does not have it yet. I do not have it in any profile I manage yet. It may change before it ships, and Google has buried better ideas than this. Nobody should rebuild their reporting this week on the strength of screenshots. But the direction of travel is information, and the direction is unmistakable: Google is investing in telling business owners what their profiles are doing, in detail, with advice attached.

Now the context that makes a dashboard redesign worth a blog post. An analysis published this month, small but pointed, tracked three established American florists through 2026 and found Google Business Profile interactions down 26 percent against the same period last year: website clicks down 30 percent, phone calls down 21 percent, direction requests down 15 percent, despite the profiles being active and well kept. Three florists are not the economy, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. But the mechanism is the one everyone in local search can see from their chair: AI answers are absorbing the simple questions before the click ever happens. The profile still matters; it increasingly matters as the source the AI reads, not as the page the customer visits. Which means the measurable actions on the profile get scarcer and more precious at the same time, and the business that cannot see its own numbers clearly is flying blind into the exact weather where visibility is hardest to earn.

That is why the new sections matter more than the new paint. Audience and search trends are the two questions every owner actually asks: who is finding me, and what were they looking for when they did? Reviews broken out in detail turns reputation from a vibe into a ledger, and reputation is half the ranking game and most of the conversion game. And recommendations on the overview page mean Google is moving from scorekeeper to coach, which owners should welcome and verify in equal measure: the coach works for Google, and its homework will tend to be Google-shaped. Do the homework. Keep your own ledger anyway.

What this means in my practice is concrete. Google Business Profiles are a core of what I sell, alongside my GBP partner Daniel Proulx, and the deal I make with clients is that the work gets reported in writing, with numbers, on a rhythm they can hold me to. A richer first-party dashboard makes that deal stronger: better baselines, cleaner before-and-after, search trends I can act on instead of guess at. When the new Performance section lands in the profiles I manage, the monthly receipts get better the same day, and I have put a full guide to reading the new reports, section by section, on christopherabraham.com and a companion for business owners at Gerris Corp.

What to do this week, test or no test. Screenshot your current Performance numbers, because baselines are the first casualty of every dashboard migration and you cannot prove growth against a number you no longer have. Keep your review replies current, because the detail view is about to make neglect visible. And when the new sections appear in your account, spend ten minutes in Audience and Search trends before you spend a dollar anywhere else. The scoreboard is being rebuilt because the game changed. Owners who learn to read the new one early will be the ones still on it.
