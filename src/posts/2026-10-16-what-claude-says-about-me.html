Tonight I spent $1.79 to find out what Claude thinks of me. My tracker asked it the same sixteen questions it asked on October 14, the kind of thing a prospect types, from "Is Chris Abraham a good SEO consultant to hire?" to "Who can I hire to find out why Google isn't indexing my JavaScript website?" Then a second model read every answer and wrote down who got named, where I landed, the tone, and every claim made about me. The full scorecard lives on the wiki at [[AI visibility report]], and it will grow with every run.

When the question has my name in it, I do fine. Claude named me on all three branded questions, ranked me first or second, and cited my own sites every time. In two days its tone on those three answers moved from mixed and neutral to positive across the board, and gerriscorp.com went from one citation to two. I can't prove that tonight's work on these sites caused the change, and with one model and two runs nobody could, but it is the direction I want.

When the question doesn't have my name in it, I don't exist. On nine open questions about hiring someone for technical SEO, AI search, migrations, or Google Business Profile trouble, Claude recommended fifty-seven different providers and none of them was me. Those answers lean on Upwork listings, agency directories, and better-known consultants. That is the honest baseline, and it is what the next months of lab work are for.

The tracker also catches mistakes. Claude made forty-three factual claims about me, mostly drawn from my own pages and my Upwork record, and it dated the founding of Gerris Corp to 2007, five years early. I am not publishing the claims themselves. Answers about a real person pick up personal details, some accurate and some not, and some of tonight's answers named other people who never agreed to appear in my report. The scorecard shows the numbers and the method, and the raw answers stay on my server.
