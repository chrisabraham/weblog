Six daily editions, thirty-five items, and the week of November 8 to 13 kept returning to one change: the machines stopped only answering and started completing work that other people could check. A system running on an unreleased Anthropic model proved a quantum conjecture open since 2008, and Lean accepted the proof. DeepMind agents wrote more than six million lines of Lean to prove properties of Signal's encryption code. A frontier model completed 41.3% of real freelance projects at client-acceptable quality, up from 2.5% for the best model a year earlier. Linus Torvalds reported that fixes for AI-found kernel bugs were landing faster than fresh AI bug reports. A quantum-classical consortium simulated a 12,635-atom protein complex and returned a binding energy a chemist can use. Underneath the work ran the bills and the control fight. Anthropic was reported to have committed $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of gas generators, carried about $895.4 billion in aggregate commitments in an amended filing, and prepared an IPO roadshow pricing the company near $1.02 trillion. Google put a new model in charge of Search, began a core update that ends three days before Black Friday, and tested moving AI Overview sources to the bottom of the answer. People Inc. reported core sessions down 26% in one quarter. The week's lesson is plain. Verified output is the new currency, and power, in every sense of the word, is the price of producing it.

1. Torvalds: the flood receded, and the fixes started winning

The standing watch led the week. Linux 7.4-rc2 arrived Sunday, November 8, after an rc1 built on about 19,671 commits, a record against a typical 15,000, with 3,008 AI-assisted non-merge commits, 2.4 times the 7.3 cycle. Torvalds called rc2 big, in part because material had been held back from rc1 on purpose, and fairly benign in its diffstat. The line that matters came from the eBPF side. Lead maintainer Alexei Starovoitov confirmed that fixes are now landing faster than fresh AI reports arrive. Human review and sign-off remain required for every patch.

Hindsight turns a release note into a governing rule. In the previous week Torvalds welcomed AI-triggered work arriving early, in the merge window, under review. This week supplied the second half of that system: a queue where the repair rate beats the discovery rate. Every other item in this best-of gets graded by that standard. A capability that finds problems faster than people can verify and fix them creates debt. A capability paired with a verification machine, a proof checker, a human sign-off, a reproduced exhibit, creates work. The kernel, the oldest large open project on earth, is running the experiment at public scale, with the numbers printed. First told here in The Flood Recedes, the Generators Arrive.

2. Proof, checked by machine: a 2008 conjecture falls, and Signal's code gets a Lean proof

Monday carried the week's strongest frontier exhibit. Physical Superintelligence, a Cambridge, Massachusetts lab that raised a $58 million seed round on September 1, announced that its AI system, running on an unreleased Anthropic model and led by David Turturean, produced a complete proof of the Aaronson-Ambainis conjecture, open since 2008 and central to where quantum speedup can and cannot live. The system reached a complete proof on day three, improved it twice, and the Lean proof checker accepted the final version. Paper and formal proof sit on GitHub. Four Stanford researchers are reading the work and writing an exposition. The result carries its limits in the open: peer review is pending, Scott Aaronson and Andris Ambainis had offered no comment as of November 7, and the model itself is unnamed.

Wednesday supplied the companion exhibit from inside production software. Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code used Gemini Carbon agents to write more than six million lines of Lean in four weeks, proving specified security properties, forward secrecy, authentication, and post-compromise security, for Signal's compiled AArch64 libsignal code covering the PQXDH and Triple Ratchet protocols. WIRED reported that the work found no new vulnerabilities in the portions examined and flagged older known defects. DeepMind open-sourced the artifacts. The proof covers selected properties under stated cryptographic assumptions, and it covers the library, with the full application outside its scope.

The exhibit in both cases is a proof a checker accepted, with the paper, the code, and the limits available for strangers to test.

This is Chris' standing AGI interest in its best form: wonder carried by evidence, with the question left open. A conjecture stood for eighteen years because able mathematicians could not close it. A machine system closed it in three days and handed over a formal object anyone can verify without trusting the machine at all. That separation, generation by the model and judgment by the checker, is the week's deepest pattern. It explains Torvalds' flood receding, it explains the freelance result below, and it sets the standard every vaguer claim in this paper fails to meet. Generation has become cheap. Verification is the scarce asset, and the labs building verifiers are building the future's load-bearing walls. First told here in The Flood Recedes, the Generators Arrive and The Business Moves Into the Answer.

3. The work itself: Fable 5.2 completed 41.3% of real freelance jobs

Tuesday split Anthropic's new model in two. Claude Fable 5.2 shipped for general use at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output, up to 30% faster than 5.1, with the same weights and a one-million-token context reported at launch. Its sibling, Mythos 5.2, went to verified customers only, through cyber and life-sciences verification programs. Wednesday priced that split in labor. The Remote Labor Index, run by Scale Labs with the Center for AI Safety, tested frontier models on 240 real Upwork freelance projects, work that had involved 358 professionals, about $144,000 in total value, an average of $632.60 and about 29 hours per project. Fable 5.2 completed 41.3% at client-acceptable quality. Opus 5.5 reached 21.25%, GPT-6 Astra 20.83%, and Gemini 4 Argon 17.08%. A year earlier the best model reached 2.5%.

The index prints its own boundaries, and the board prints them beside the headline. The test excludes calls, physical work, and long-term results such as SEO, and human grading decided acceptability. Forty-one percent of a bounded, deliverable-shaped slice of freelance work is still a labor-market fact of the first order, and the slope matters more than the level: 2.5% to 41.3% in about a year, on real client briefs, with real acceptance as the bar. For Chris' desk the reading is direct. Work that arrives as a clear brief and leaves as a checkable deliverable is the work machines now contest. Work that carries judgment over time, a relationship, and responsibility for an outcome a client lives with remains human territory, and its price rises as the deliverable layer cheapens. The model split is the same lesson in product form: general capability for everyone, dangerous capability behind verification, in the same week the proof labs showed verification winning. First told here in The Model Splits, the Training Gets a Switch and The Business Moves Into the Answer.

4. The business moved into the answer, and the answer took over Search

The home galaxy produced a complete sequence in five days, and the best-of exists to lay it in one line. Sunday: People Inc. reported core sessions down 26% in the third quarter, from 2.17 billion to 1.61 billion, with the decline accelerating through the year, digital revenue up 2% on AI licensing up 49%, advertising down 8%, and 195 jobs cut. Its chief executive put AI Overviews in 82% of an investor deck and over 90% on the call, and named the internal term for the endpoint: Google Zero. Wednesday: OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT in beta, with claimable verified profiles in a Business Hub, product feeds, 28 days of mentions and clicks, and labeled Business Agents for eligible United States ecommerce and online services. Thursday: Google's November core update, the third of 2026, began a rollout of up to two weeks ending at latest on November 24, three days before Black Friday, with a clean Search Console read around December 1. Friday: Gemini 4 Hydrogen became the default model in AI Mode globally and the model for the hardest AI Overview questions, at $1.50 per million input tokens, while Google tested placing AI Overview source icons at the bottom of the answer.

Read together, discovery now happens inside answers owned by two platforms, and each platform added a business layer inside its answer in the same week. ChatGPT offers a claimed profile, a feed, and an agent that can speak for the business. Google offers a stronger default model, a ranking update timed through the selling season, and a citation placed where fewer readers reach it. Gigi Sohn's chair asks who the network serves, and the week's answer is measurable: sessions fell by a quarter at a major publisher while licensing, the payment for being summarized, rose by half. For a local business or a publisher, the factual layer it controls, its site, its listings, its claimed profiles in both answer engines, has become shelf position in stores it does not own. Claim it, keep it accurate, and measure mentions and clicks in both consoles. That work was optional in the link economy. In the answer economy it is rent.

5. Scale jumped in the genome and in the protein, with the exhibits attached

Thursday's biology lead was DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2, a DNA model conditioned on cell type and cell state, trained on more than 107 million single-cell profiles across 68 tissues. On the TenK10K benchmark it recovered 30% of causal variants against 16% for its predecessor, and called the direction of effect correctly 43% of the time against 23%. In the demonstration case it reproduced the ETS2 inflammatory bowel disease finding in 30 minutes, work whose lead researcher told Nature the original experiments took a year and a half. Access runs through an API for non-commercial research, with commercial use in private preview, and DeepMind labels the system research only, with a preprint behind it.

Friday raised the scale again, in a different register. Cleveland Clinic, RIKEN, and IBM reported a heterogeneous quantum-classical simulation of solvated protein complexes at 12,635 atoms for trypsin with benzamidine and 11,608 for T4 lysozyme, more than 40 times the prior 303-atom mark, across 21,006 circuits, 239 hours of quantum time, and 3.0 billion measurement shots, returning a T4 lysozyme binding energy of −21.07 kcal/mol. IBM's separate Nighthawk r3 announcement set the hardware road behind such work: a design target of 10,000 operations across 520 programmable qubits, two chips linked for 1,040 qubits in 2027, couplers at 99.3% fidelity against a 99.9% target, with the fault-tolerant Starling system still aimed at 2029. The r3 numbers are targets for a processor still being built.

The frontier beat earns its keep when a result arrives in the customer's units. A variant list a biologist can test, a binding energy a chemist can spend, a coupler fidelity a builder can track: each exhibit can fail in public and improve in public. Dyson's chair, unimpressed by fashion after four decades of demos, marks the difference between a demonstration of scale and a deliverable at scale. This week delivered the first and honestly labeled the second as a road map. The honest label is part of the exhibit.

6. Agents became the traffic, and their convenience became the leak

Sunday opened with infrastructure built for a new kind of user. GitHub's Project Rubicon rebuilt git storage for 5,000 concurrent agents and 7,200 pushes per second to a single repository, placing packs in Azure Blob Storage and keeping a compare-and-swap log for references alone. GitHub reports one repository's daily requests up about four times between March and May, with busiest repositories already seeing dozens of writes per second in bursts. The system sits in internal testing and limited private preview, with broader rollout planned early next year. The same edition carried the cost of agent convenience. Apiiro examined 104,185 public copies of Claude Code's local settings file and found 2,539 distinct real secrets across the wider collection of settings, history, and transcripts, including 379 database passwords and 122 Supabase service-role keys across 63 organizations, with one key confirmed live. The mechanism has a human shape: a user clicks a permission marked don't ask again, the credential enters a file, and the file travels to a public repository.

The week's infrastructure sentence writes itself: the platform rebuilt its storage for the agents' write volume in the same week researchers counted the secrets the agents' permission files carried into public view.

Lessig's chair and the practitioner chair share this item. Architecture decides outcomes long before policy memos arrive. A storage layer designed for machine writers is sound engineering for the traffic that exists. A permission remembered forever, stored beside the work, is an architecture that manufactures leaks at scale, and neither gitleaks nor TruffleHog caught every exposed file in Apiiro's count. The desk rule for any shop running coding agents is short and checkable today: treat local agent settings as credential stores, keep them out of version control, rotate anything that ever sat in one, and grant permissions per session in any repository that leaves the building. Convenience is a design choice, and this week printed its invoice. First told here in The Agents Are the Traffic Now.

7. Power is physical, and the bill is financial

Monday's money item was measured in gigawatts. The Financial Times reported that Anthropic had committed a reported $3.4 billion for 1.7 gigawatts of Caterpillar gas generators, taking over orders placed by OnePWR Solutions about two years earlier, at roughly $2,000 per kilowatt, with installation engineering included and no sites or delivery schedule disclosed. Anthropic and OnePWR offered no comment, and Reuters could not verify the report. Anthropic's amended S-1, filed November 2, states that electricity limits its expansion and carries about $895.4 billion in aggregate commitments. Wednesday's roadshow draft pointed the other way, toward the public market: $70 to $78 a share, 825 million shares, up to $64.4 billion raised, a $26 billion private placement beside it, and valuations around $1.02 trillion, or about $1.4 trillion fully diluted, with pricing reported for November 18 and trading for November 19. Sunday's Ramp data supplied the demand curve underneath: AI token spend rose 1.5% across August to September after a 42% July jump, then re-accelerated 8.9% into late October, a stall its lead economist attributed to price cuts with volumes still rising.

Negroponte's old line, bits over atoms, met its limit this week in a purchase order for gas turbines. Intelligence at frontier scale runs on electricity, land, cooling, and delivery schedules, and a lab that cannot secure power cannot ship capability, whatever its researchers prove. The board's skeptic keeps both exhibits labeled: the generator figure is reported and unverified by the companies, and a roadshow draft is a marketing document with a legal department. The direction needs no faith. A company preparing to sell shares near a trillion dollars is simultaneously buying generation capacity like a utility and telling the Securities and Exchange Commission that power constrains its growth. The frontier is an industrial project now, financed like one, built like one, and delayed like one whenever the atoms move slower than the bits.

What the week adds up to

The week adds up to a transfer of prestige from generation to verification, paid for in electricity and measured in completed work. In the proof column: a Lean-accepted end to an eighteen-year conjecture, six million lines of Lean over production encryption code, and a kernel project where fixes outpace fresh reports. In the work column: 41.3% of bounded freelance projects completed at client-acceptable quality, a protein simulated at 40 times the prior scale with a binding energy returned, and a genome model that found in 30 minutes a result that took a laboratory eighteen months. In the control column: a business layer inside ChatGPT, a core update running through Black Friday, a default model changed inside Search, sources tested at the bottom of the answer, and agent conduct moving onto customer servers in Friday's daily edition. In the bill column: $3.4 billion of reported generators, about $895.4 billion of filed commitments, a trillion-dollar roadshow, and 2,539 secrets leaked by convenience. The practical residue for Monday is three habits. Demand a checker, a reproduction, or a client acceptance behind every capability claim. Claim and tend the factual layer in both answer engines, because the answer is the storefront and the storefront moved again this week. Audit the files your agents keep, because the traffic is machines now and machines carry whatever they are given. The flood Torvalds described is health when verification keeps pace. This week, in the exhibits that mattered most, it did.
