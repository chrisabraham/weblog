AI now writes the three most tedious fields in SEO, image alt text, page titles, and meta descriptions, and after testing what is actually on the market, here is my whole verdict in the first paragraph, where verdicts belong. For image alt text on WordPress, buy AltText.ai, the credit based service, and run it as a describer. For alt text on a site builder, Duda's built in generator beat everything I have tried, perfect score, no credits, no plugin. For titles and descriptions in the editor on a WordPress site, Yoast SEO Premium generates them where you already work, but Yoast writes no alt text at all, which remains the strangest gap in the business. For bulk titles and descriptions across hundreds of pages, SEOPress PRO is the workhorse. If a site already runs Rank Math, its Content AI does the same job without changing stacks. And the wildcard, a free plugin called AI SEO Tools, does both jobs on your own API key for fractions of a cent. Six tools, three fields, and one rule that decides whether any of them help or harm: the generator that describes wins, the generator that stuffs loses. Reviews first, then the rule.

AltText.ai, for image alt text on WordPress

This is the service I remembered and went looking for, and it is the category standard for a reason. One credit writes one image's alt text. The WordPress plugin backfills an entire media library in bulk, then writes alt text on every new upload, which is the moment the work should happen. Pricing checked this fall runs from $49 a year for 1,200 credits at the Bronze tier, $189 for 6,000 at Silver, $489 for 24,000 at Gold, with one time packs of 50 credits for $3 if you want to test it on a real library before subscribing, and credits roll over instead of expiring. There is an agency shaped tier for running many client sites off one account. Two warnings. Advanced formats like AVIF and SVG bill double, so a performance tuned library costs more than the headline rate. And its proudest advertised feature, pulling your Yoast or Rank Math focus keyword into the alt text, is the stuffing habit I argue against below, so buy it for the describing and govern the keyword feature with your own hand. Verdict: the alt text tool to buy on WordPress.

Duda's built in alt generator, the one to copy

My single best alt text experience was not a plugin at all. On Duda, the website builder, the automatic AI alt generation wrote descriptions for a client's images at the moment they landed on the page, plain descriptions of what the photographs showed, and when the accessibility and SEO checkers ran, the site scored a perfect score on its image alt text. I could not have improved the batch by hand, and I tried to find fault with it out of professional pride. No credits, no settings page, no ceremony. Platform builders can beat plugins at this because they meet the image at the door, before it is published naked and forgotten in a library of ten thousand. Verdict: if your site lives on Duda, you already own the best alt text tool I have used. Everyone building these tools should copy its manners.

Yoast SEO Premium, for titles and descriptions, and the gap

Yoast Premium, at about $119 a year, added AI generation for SEO titles and meta descriptions inside the editor, and it is genuinely the right surface: the suggestion arrives in the exact box you were about to type into, in the plugin most of my world already runs. I am a Yoast expert and Yoast stays on my stack; expertise is not zero sum. But Yoast generates no alt text, and I want the question on the record, because I have asked it privately for a year: why hasn't Yoast added this yet? Yoast already inspects every image. It already warns you when an alt attribute is empty. It already owns the tired editor moment where a drafted description would be accepted with gratitude. The vision models are a commodity now, cheaper than the text models Yoast already calls. My guess, printed as a guess, is organizational, not technical: alt text is claimed by the accessibility conscience and the SEO checklist at the same time, and work owned by two teams ships under neither, while a credit based specialist and a site builder walked through the open door. Verdict: buy Premium for the titles and descriptions if you live in Yoast, and keep asking Yoast about the alt text.

SEOPress PRO, for bulk titles and descriptions

When the job is not one page but a thousand empty title and description fields on a client site, the editor by editor tools are the wrong shape and SEOPress PRO is the right one: $49 a year per site, true bulk generation across post types, and your choice of billing, your own AI provider key or their managed credits. Bulk is a different discipline from drafting. You need prompts that carry your rules, batches you can sample before committing, and a tool that does not fall over at page four hundred. Verdict: the bulk worker, and the one I reach for on programmatic and location page work.

Rank Math Content AI, if the site already runs Rank Math

Rank Math's built in AI does the same generation, titles, descriptions, and other maintenance tasks, priced in its own credits inside the plugin, including bulk meta generation for selected items. I do not migrate a healthy site's SEO plugin to chase a generator, and neither should you; stack loyalty is not a strategy, but stack stability is. If the site already runs Rank Math, Content AI is the path of least resistance, and least resistance is a legitimate engineering argument. Verdict: use it where it already lives.

AI SEO Tools, the free wildcard

The last one is a free WordPress.org plugin called AI SEO Tools, and it fits my economics so well I trial it second despite it being the newest name here. It does both jobs, alt text and meta, writes them into the fields of whichever SEO plugin you already run, Yoast, Rank Math, SEOPress, and runs on your own API key to OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, or OpenRouter, so an image costs fractions of a cent instead of a credit, and a spend cap stops a bulk run before it surprises you. It also ships command line commands, which turns a thousand image backfill into a script I can run and log instead of a subscription I rent forever. Verdict: unproven at the others' scale, priced like the future, worth a supervised trial on a real library.

The one rule: describe, do not stuff

Every tool above can be run two ways, and the way decides everything. Run as a describer, the generator writes what the image shows and stops, and the alt text serves its actual reader, the person who cannot see the picture. My whole test for this is old and low technology: read the page through Lynx, the text browser, the way a screen reader's owner meets it, and listen. A description sounds like a description. A stuffed alt sounds like a billboard that wandered into the wrong attribute, brand name, keyword, and slogan wrapped around a photograph of a man holding a wrench, and keyword stuffing in the one attribute built for blind readers is a small cruelty wearing an SEO hat. The same discipline governs the titles and descriptions these tools draft. My fences: titles around sixty characters, descriptions around one hundred sixty, the brand name at home on the home, about, and contact pages and out of the category pages where buyers search by what you do and not by who you are, and no pipes, dashes, or em dashes as joinery in a title that should read like a sentence. Some of that can be mechanized, templates that hold the brand off, generators set to fill only empty fields, counters that warn. The judgment itself cannot be set into any settings page I have ever opened, because no settings page knows the phrase your buyer types at midnight. So: buy the tools, set the fences, sample the output, and read the page the way the reader who needs it most will meet it.

That is the whole toolbox. AltText.ai for WordPress alt text, Duda's built in generator as the manners to copy, Yoast Premium for titles and descriptions in the editor with one loud unanswered question about alt, SEOPress PRO for bulk, Rank Math's AI where Rank Math already lives, and a free bring your own key wildcard for the script minded. Three fields, six tools, one rule: describe, do not stuff.
