Reuters Trading Day: remember, remember, the fifth of November

Reuters opened its Trading Day account with the Guy Fawkes rhyme, and the rhyme earned its place. About $2.5 trillion came off the Nasdaq in one session. S&P 500 companies shed about $2.9 trillion, a single-day record. The Magnificent Seven alone lost about $1.04 trillion. The VIX rose 69% to about 28.18. Figures at that scale describe a single position unwinding: long the buildout, at any price.

CNN: the tech-heavy Nasdaq plunges

The names read like the buildout's org chart. Nvidia fell 9.26% to $181.60 and gave back its gains for the year, with its quarterly update due November 17. AMD fell 17.26% to about $404.40, two days after guiding above expectations. Broadcom fell 10.43%. SanDisk fell 22%. Micron and Marvell fell about 17% each. Qualcomm fell 9.5% on Wednesday's miss, when handset revenue took the cost of memory with it. The GPU cloud names fell hardest per dollar of revenue: CoreWeave down 10% to 14%, Nebius down 15% to 17.6%. Tesla fell 11% and SpaceX fell 9% to 10%. Meta fell 3.4%. That last figure is the tell in the whole table. The company that owns capacity and runs it for its own products fell a third as far as the companies that build, finance, and rent capacity to everyone else.

What got repriced

Tuesday's midterms did this. Democrats took both chambers of Congress. A Democrat won the governorship of Texas, the state where Google, Meta, Oracle, and OpenAI are building their largest sites. Voters in ten states rejected most of the local data-center measures on their ballots. A chip works the same on Thursday as it did on Monday, and demand for compute did not fall 12% between breakfast and the close. What fell was the market's price for a political assumption: that county commissions, statehouses, and Congress would keep saying yes to the buildout at the speed the valuations required. The sell-off landed on ground softened by AI capital spending running ahead of cash flow, credit extended against GPU clouds, the 10-year yield near 5%, and rate-hike expectations pushed out to March. Politics supplied the match. The leverage was already stacked.

One trade, 48 hours

Chris reads the two sessions as one trade worked over 48 hours. Wednesday bought the resolution, up 1,569 points. Thursday sold the consequences, down 1,833. His stated belief, in his words, is that the only reason Donald J. Trump exists is to add volatility to the market so more of his Overlords can make “buy dip” “sell high” day trade billions. On that reading the product was never the policy. The product is the swing.

The local branch of the same office

The author spent part of the same afternoon at the Arlington Public Library, where the building's web filter had filed his newspaper as a suspicious new domain and reset his podcast outright. His consultancy sites loaded fine. He holds no grudge against the filter; it processed his papers and returned its ruling, which is more ceremony than the market gave Nvidia. Permission layers all the way down, and every layer keeps a clerk.

Friday brings the next reading at 0830 ET, the October jobs report, and The Retest's closing brief carries the full tape. The chips still work. The models still train. The question the market started asking on Thursday is older than the technology: who gets to build, where, and at whose say-so. Prices will follow the answer.
