Blackbox, usually typed bb, is the secretary Chris Abraham built for himself. It runs on [[Pinebox]], he reaches it over SSH from his ThinkPads, and he talks to it in plain sentences. It keeps his tasks, the minutes of his day, his diary, his Upwork pipeline, and his unread mail in one place, and tells him what he is about to forget. The design brief was a good first lieutenant: someone who knows the schedule and the promises and doesn't try to be a friend. The full command list is on [[Blackbox commands]].

What it does

Tasks

Todoist is the official list. Blackbox reads it, changes it when asked, and keeps a permanent local log of every task it has seen and every change, whether made through bb or in the Todoist app. Tasks get short stable numbers, so bb 12 done always means the same task. Anything labeled @bb in Todoist on the phone becomes a minute on the next sync.

Minutes

Plain sentences such as "I decided…", "I owe Alice…", "Bob is blocked until…" are recorded as minutes with no AI call. Nothing is deleted: a correction supersedes an entry and "scratch that" strikes one, but the original stays.

Briefs and the diary

bb good morning and bb good night each gather the day's record and make one model call, costing a few cents. The night close writes a diary entry to ~/text/diary, append-only, with no approval step. If Chris forgets to say good night, a timer closes the day at 11:45 PM.

What am I forgetting

A ranked list built from Blackbox's own records with no AI call: a failing backup, people who owe something for five days or more, promises with no task, long blocks, tasks pushed back three or more times, duplicates, and unsaved drafts. Each item comes with the command that fixes it.

Email, inbox, and calendar

Blackbox drafts email in Chris' voice, but it never sends: bb send opens Alpine's compose screen already filled in, and the mail goes only when he presses Ctrl+X. It reads unread Gmail and the Google calendar through its own sign-in, read-only, and leaves payment, invoice, and pitch mail out of its reports.

Upwork

Through Upwork's official MCP server, Blackbox pulls new jobs, ranks them against Chris' lanes, and gives each a verdict from his playbook (see [[Upwork verdicts]]). It drafts proposals and screening answers in plain ASCII, checks them for length and placeholders, and never submits anything.

Continuity

Added on October 16, 2026, and written by OpenAI's Codex. Projects collect linked sources into a dossier, checkpoints record where work stopped, bb I have 25 minutes picks one task that fits and drafts the first piece, decisions can carry dated tripwires, scope can carry a cap in minutes, and an evidence bank holds claims Chris has checked before any proposal may cite them.

Design rules

  • Plain English first. Command words are ordinary speech unless the rest of the line fits the command.
  • Misfires are loud. A short command-like line that matches nothing gets a warning instead of quietly going to the chat, because Chris types and moves on without checking.
  • Every screen ends with ready-to-type next: lines.
  • Nothing leaves without a yes. No sending, submitting, or spending on its own.
  • Records are append-only, and database changes only ever add tables, so an older bb still opens a newer database.
  • One model per job, with fallbacks and a local cost ledger (bb cost).

Operations

Three timers run on [[Pinebox]]: a keepalive at 6:45 and 18:45 that renews the Upwork and Google sign-ins, the 11:45 PM day close, and a backup at about 3:30 AM that encrypts the database and writing folders, decrypts them again to verify, and pushes the snapshot to a private repository, keeping fourteen nights. The test suite runs the real command against fake Todoist, Upwork, and model servers in a throwaway home folder.

Lessons from early operation

The first weeks of use established the value of ending a briefing with a few immediately usable next: commands. A result that names an actual next action can bridge the gap between understanding work and starting it. The October status report also documented integration tests against fake services and a local record independent of a model's passing answer. The task manager remains the task authority; proposed external actions wait for human approval.
