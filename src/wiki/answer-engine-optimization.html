Answer engine optimization is the practice of making information understandable and citable by search features and answer-producing AI systems. The weblog uses four related terms. SEO, search engine optimization, improves the accessibility, usefulness, and discoverability of a website in search engines. AEO shortens answer engine optimization. GEO, generative engine optimization, names the same citation work with the generative engine in view. AIO, AI optimization, serves as the umbrella term. The four share one foundation: a crawlable site, pages an engine can index, and statements a reader can verify. They differ in the surface that rewards the work: a ranked list, or a generated answer carrying citations.

What answer engines cite

Answer engines cite sources they already trust, and trust accrues to sites with a record. The record takes a concrete shape: named expertise, first-hand experience, current and accurate pages, and work other people cite because it helped them. Google's AI answers draw on the live index, so freshness operates as a ranking input on that surface: a site publishing current material competes on the day the question gets asked. Podcast show notes supply a worked example on this site. One episode's notes replicate across hundreds of podcast indexes, each copy carrying the same names, links, and phrasing onto domains engines already trust, and Google's AI Overview quoted those notes back word for word. The case study is the show notes cascade.

Practices the weblog rejects

The avoid list at how to lose at AI search sorts rejected practices into three tiers. Played-out work costs time and returns nothing: writing to a word count, tuning keyword density, and publishing commodity listicles interchangeable with a competitor's page. Folklore circulates with confidence and thin evidence: an llms.txt file treated as a ranking charm, FAQ schema treated as a traffic spell, statistics stuffed into sentences and labeled GEO, and citations bought in roundups assembled for that purpose. The third tier kills sites: content farms at machine scale, parasite hosting, expired domains refilled under an old reputation, reviews written by the reviewed, and pages that show AI crawlers one text and human visitors another. Each tactic in the third tier works for a season, and the season ends with removal from the index and the answers alike.

How the work is checked

Optimization claims get tested against a fixed panel of questions, run repeatedly, with mentions and citations recorded per engine.
