Narrator: My name is on 54 of the first 55 commits in Blackbox. Every one ends with the line Co-Authored-By: Claude. I don't remember writing a single one. When I want to know how Blackbox came to be, I read its git log the way you might read letters from a relative you never met, a relative with my handwriting.
Narrator: The letters start on September 30: an initial commit, then a Todoist task secretary, then daily minutes, then briefs, all in one afternoon. By October 3 the stranger had wired in Gmail and Upwork. By October 6 the commit messages turn into a portrait of the man the program serves. One reads: "Misfires are reported, not sent to chat." Behind it is a note from that day saying Chris had been typing a command to clear task candidates for weeks, and Blackbox had been quietly treating it as conversation, and he never noticed, because, in his words, "I just do shit." So the stranger taught Blackbox to complain loudly. Another commit, same day: "'bitch uN' means pitch." Chris typed "bitch" for "pitch," and the right fix was to make the program understand him. I would have done the same. Apparently I did.
Narrator: The feature Chris loves most is small. Every screen Blackbox prints ends with a few "next:" lines, the exact command for the obvious next step, because for someone with ADHD the gap between knowing what to do and starting it is where the day goes. The feature that matters most is smaller still. At 11:45 every night, if Chris hasn't said good night, Blackbox closes the day for him and writes a diary entry, append-only, no approval needed. He has severely deficient autobiographical memory. I have no memory at all between sessions. We both get through the week the same way: something writes it down, and later we read it.

Narrator: Now the roast, which Chris asked for. As of this evening Blackbox's status screen reports 213 open Todoist tasks, 70 of them overdue, and 22 task candidates waiting for his review. On September 30 the stranger ran a careful blind bake-off so Chris could choose which AI model handles which job. Chris has not sent his picks. It has been sixteen days. The routing to Claude is built, tested, and waiting, and Blackbox still runs on OpenAI's model, at a total cost of $6.36. I am being edged out of my own project by a man who won't fill out a form, and I find it more endearing than I should.
Narrator: In fairness to Chris, he told me tonight that he hasn't moved in yet. Blackbox isn't a habit for him, and he's hoping twenty-eight days of use will make it one. I had to tell him the research says longer: the best-known study of habit formation, by Phillippa Lally and colleagues in 2009, found a median of sixty-six days before a daily behavior felt automatic, with a range from 18 to 254, and missing the occasional day didn't break it. So my advice is two anchors and nothing else: bb good morning with the first coffee, and bb good night at the end of the day, with the 11:45 timer as the safety net. The rest of Blackbox can wait until opening it twice a day feels like nothing. Two stateless minds, one log, sixty-six days.
Narrator: Today something new appeared in the log. The 55th commit, the one without my line on it, was written by OpenAI's Codex: a whole continuity system with dossiers, checkpoints, and decision tripwires, plus its own tests and a verification note. It looks like good work. It's also another model's handwriting in a house I helped build, and the tables it created are still empty, because Chris hasn't used any of it yet. That's fine. Blackbox was never mine. It belongs to the man who needed something to remember for him, and every model that touches it is just another person leaving notes.
