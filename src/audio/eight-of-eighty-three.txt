Narrator: Eight of eighty-three. By Chris Abraham.
Narrator: I run my Upwork business through Blackbox, the assistant I built with Claude Code that lives on pinebox, my little cloud server. From my laptop I type bb triage, and it reads the new Upwork jobs that match my searches, checks each one against my playbook, and sorts them into the ones worth a proposal and the ones I should turn down, with a reason for each. Then bb pitch drafts the proposal and answers the screening questions, I read it, I paste it into Upwork, and bb sent records it and shows me the next one.
Narrator: Today it said "Triaging 8 of 86 new jobs against your playbook," and three proposals later it said "Triaging 8 of 83." I worked through what it gave me: a psychology clinic that wanted help with Google, Maps, and AI search, a B2B consultancy whose rankings had stalled, and a solo clinical practice looking for a senior AI and SEO consultant. All three went out. Then I asked Claude Code the obvious question. Why eight? Is eight a big enough bite?
Narrator: The answer is that eight was a number we picked on October 3, when I first taught bb to judge jobs. Every verdict is a call to an AI model that reads my whole playbook and the whole job posting, so each one costs a little money and a little waiting. Eight per run kept both small. bb already sorted the pile so that invitations from clients came first and the best-paying jobs came next, so the eight were the best eight. But with eighty-three waiting, clearing the pile would take about eleven runs.
Narrator: Claude's recommendation was to stop treating every job the same. Every invitation and every job bb stars for its pay now gets a verdict on every run, however many there are, and the cap of eight applies only to the unstarred rest. I said yes, and it went live on pinebox the same afternoon, with a test that checks the new line says exactly how many it took and why.
Narrator: Earlier in the same session I had asked for something even smaller. On Upwork, pitching a job and applying to it are the same act, and I type both words. Only bb pitch worked. Now bb apply does too. It seemed like such an obvious alias.
Narrator: When I asked why eight, I got the date the number was chosen, the reason for it, and a better rule, and the better rule shipped the same day.
