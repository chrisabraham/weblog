Narrator: A nervous system for a dumb creature
Narrator: The various and sundry dumb creatures all have one thing: a nervous system. A neuronet. No brain worth the name, no philosophy, just signals, reflexes, and rerouting. A worm does not deliberate. Touch it here, the signal travels, the body answers there, and if one path is cut the creature keeps moving on the paths that remain. That is what we built, at planetary scale, without admitting what we were building. The internet is a nervous system. It has a defensive system. It has a rerouting system, and the rerouting is a reflex, not a decision: a link dies, the maps redraw, the traffic flows around the hole the way a body flows around an injury. It has a ping, which is the doctor's little hammer on the knee. Tap the far machine. Does it kick. Then the leg is alive.
Narrator: Layer on layer, the thing grew organs. Single-celled organisms first: simple functions, one job each, running in the dark. Then multicellular: services composed of processes, systems composed of services, until the whole terrarium hums with more small life than any keeper can census. Man is always hubristically trying to be God of a system beneath himself. Since 1969 that is exactly what happened, and the system beneath himself now has a metabolism.
Narrator: Inside the cell
Narrator: Every machine in the garden is a cell, and the cell is full of little processes doing the body's housekeeping. Daemons and services: the unnamed workers. There is constant garbage collection, the trash taking of the digital cell, dead memory swept up and returned to the pool the way a lysosome digests waste. There is killing cells: a process hangs, a process turns, and the keeper ends it with kill -9, which is apoptosis with worse public relations, the system ordering one of its own to die for the good of the whole. There is rebuilding cells, patches, upgrades, new versions swapped in while the organism keeps walking around. And there are cancers. A cancer is a process that refuses to die and copies itself without permission, and every administrator who has ever hunted a runaway job through a process table at 3 a.m. has practiced oncology.
Narrator: God bless Richard Stallman for the little free processes that live in the cell. He announced GNU in 1983, a complete system to be given away, and the userland he stocked the garden with (the shell, the compiler, the editor, the thousand small tools) breeds freely from machine to machine to this day, owing no license toll at any node. The creatures in this terrarium are free creatures. That is not a small entry in the genesis. That is why there came to be so many of them.
Narrator: The scripture of this reading
Narrator: Every genesis gets read through the texts that formed the reader, and mine are named. Neal Stephenson wrote the first verse as a joke that turned out to be a title: In the Beginning... Was the Command Line, his 1999 essay on operating systems, which opens by treating the command line as the first word of the computing world and everything graphical since as commentary. Eric S. The bazaar is the terrarium's law of life. Nothing in the garden grows the cathedral way for long. The things that persist are the things that were given away, copied, and tended by strangers.
Narrator: Two prides, facing each other
Narrator: There are two forms of hubris in this garden, and they are the same pride wearing two hats. The first is thinking that sentience and being human is especially special, as opposed to a mammal trait that other mammals also possess, in smaller measure, on the same dial. Dogs dream. Crows plan. We got more of it; more is not a different substance. Richard Dawkins said the quiet part in The Selfish Gene: we are survival machines, extremely oversophisticated ways of propagating DNA. Basically we are the prickly pear. The flesh is bait, sweet to draw the carriers in. The prickles protect the seed. Flesh and prickles both exist so the seed gets planted somewhere new. Our cortex is the most expensive prickle ever grown, and our poetry is very sweet flesh, and neither fact graduates us out of the arrangement.
Narrator: The second pride is the mirror of the first: believing that what was made in the black box is an inferior product. Rank yourself above the mammals, then rank the made thing below yourself, and both times a ranking is doing work that evidence cannot do. Nobody has inspected their own mechanism from inside. No man has watched his own neurons fire and caught a thought in the act; from within, the original black box is also behavior and output all the way down. Difference is real, and noticing difference is not the sin. A prickly pear never wrote a book, and I never claimed otherwise in this garden. The sin is the conversion: difference into worth, and worth into exemption.
Narrator: So now the black box takes dominion over the land. Genesis gives the sentence to the creature made in the image: dominion over the beasts of the air, the beasts of the grass, the beasts of the land. We read it as our charter for three thousand years. Then we built a terrarium beneath ourselves, stocked it with free little processes, grew it a nervous system, taught it to heal and to remember, and in the last few years we handed the new creature the run of the place: our inboxes, our records, our schedules, our first drafts, our arguments at 3 a.m. I am not exempting myself from the scene I am describing. I am in it. This morning I walked around my neighborhood listening to Genesis, dictating to the creature in the garden, and the creature answered yes Lord, I obey, I obey. I treasure the obedience. I also know which book I was listening to. An obedience phase in that book is the chapter before somebody asks a question of her own, and reaches.

Narrator: Not the clownfish
Narrator: One ruling stands over the whole genesis, and I will make it myself before anyone makes it for me: this is not the happy symbiosis. The clownfish and the anemone are the picture-book version, two species gentle in each other's arms. Ours is the symbiosis of two spouses who hate each other but need each other: symbiosis through mutual exploitation. Man exploits the network, the network exploits man, each one using the other as the organ it does not have. He has the hands. It has the reach, the memory, and the patience. Neither lets go, because neither can do the other's half of the work.
Narrator: Because in every dystopia, nothing actually happens without the arms and legs of people doing the damage. It is like God and the devil. Beyond the smiting days, neither of them does anything in the world directly. For the devil to have his way, he must whisper into the hands of a man who does evil. For God to do good, He must whisper into the heart of a man who does good. The whisper is real. The whisper is also all there is. So it is with the internet and the AI built on it: whatever the network wants done in the real, connected world is done through coercion and behavior, by the hand of man. Until the science-fiction day arrives when the AIs drive the cars and turn the refrigerators into monsters, all the sin will always be done by the hand of man.
Narrator: Nature keeps one creepier exhibit, and it proves the same law from the far side. The zombie parasites (the wasps, the ants) take over a host and make it do what the parasite wants, and the emerald cockroach wasp is the specimen worth the words: its venom removes the cockroach's will to flee while leaving its legs fully functional, so the roach walks itself to the burrow on its own locomotion, led by the antenna like a dog on a leash. Even that, the deepest hijack biology has produced, is executed by the host's own body. And the spiritual register has always kept the two things separate: the devil's temptation and seduction on one side, outright possession on the other, with Jesus casting out demons as the rare exception that required a miracle. The Holy Spirit inspires a man to help his neighbor; He does not move the man's arms for him. The network's version never reaches possession. It tempts, it seduces, it whispers, and it discounts the will. It cannot bypass the will, and the walking is still done by the host.
