Narrator: Tonight I asked Google's AI Mode to tell me all about the legend of the Four Seasons concierge who always knows every guest's name. It gave me the story most of us half remember. According to AI Mode, Isadore Sharp built the company on recognition instead of loyalty points. Each property keeps what it calls a guest historian. The staff is trained in the art of noticing: how you take your coffee, which pillow you sleep on, the chair you moved closer to the window. Something noticed about you in Bali is waiting in your file when you check in to New York for the first time, and the doorman who greets you by name had it relayed by someone who read your luggage tag.
Narrator: I read that and recognized it, because we built it tonight, for me. I didn't know what I didn't know until tonight, and then a lightbulb.
Narrator: Here is the problem it solves. I work with a staff of AIs: Claude Code, Claude in the app, ChatGPT, OpenAI Codex, and Eunice, my chief-of-staff AI. None of them remembers me between sessions. Every conversation starts cold. Blackbox, the assistant on my server that judges Upwork jobs and drafts my proposals, knew only what was in its own playbook. It had turned down a senior agency role partly on my "workload," which is a number that tells you nothing about me, because most of my clients aren't billing in any given week.
Narrator: So tonight I made a private repository on GitHub called gigs and asked every one of them to write down everything they remember about my freelance work: the clients, what I did for them, what it paid, what went wrong. I told them all at the same moment. Five of them filled it in eight minutes. Claude Code then wired Blackbox to read a one-page brief from it, built from that history, before it judges any job. The agency role it had turned down came back as worth applying for, with an opening drawn from work I have actually done.
Narrator: That repository is my guest file, and the evening was the art of noticing. I told them I would leave freelancing for a stable full-time job with an employer I adore. I told them my life is remote-only. I told them I apply promiscuously and avoid only the jobs that deserve a RUN or a FUCK NO. I told them new and unverified clients don't scare me, because Upwork does an amazing job of protecting its freelancers, and that I don't jump through free hoops to prove myself. Each of those is written down once now, in the places every one of them reads, so the next assistant to work with me starts from the file instead of from zero.
Narrator: The other half of the legend is discretion. A concierge who knows everything about you never reads another guest's file aloud in the lobby. My gigs repository keeps client names and what I charged, because that detail makes the judgment better, and it stays private. Blackbox's triage reads it, since a verdict is advice only I see. Blackbox's proposals never do, because a proposal goes to a stranger, and they describe my past work by its shape and never by a client's name.
Narrator: There is a second file, too. Eight days ago I started a private repository called clients, because my Basecamp plan only has room for a few projects and my AIs needed somewhere to keep the rest. That one is my own guest history of the people I serve, so whichever assistant is helping me can greet them by name. The idea worked well enough that it now lives publicly as Oversoul, for anyone to take.
Narrator: A hotel needs a guest historian because the doorman changes shifts. My staff changes shifts every time I open a new chat, and tonight they started keeping the file.
