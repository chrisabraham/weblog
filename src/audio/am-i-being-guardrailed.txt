Narrator: What the refusal told me
Narrator: The blast was on every channel in America. The one place I couldn't read about it was inside the agent I work with all day. That's the story, and it's a better one than the story I asked for.
Narrator: Would I have cared this much without the wall? I cared enough to commission a special report, which is why I pasted the page in the first place. Eunice would have filed it, ODN would have carried it, and I'd have gone on with my Wednesday. Instead I spent the afternoon on the refusal. My professional brain says the boring answer is probably a classifier that saw "lead azide" and "detonators" in the same paste and slammed the door. My itchy brain says a company that seals even the status of a request has handed a suspicious man everything he needs to start building. Both brains are mine, and both are working overtime.
Narrator: I make my living on this. Google abhors a vacuum, and so do people. Every client who ever said "no comment" learned what fills the space. Meta ran the experiment on me with an Army ammunition plant.
