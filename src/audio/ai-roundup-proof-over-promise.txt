Narrator: OpenAI: one campaign found 948 unique Linux privilege-escalation flaws
Narrator: OpenAI described the work on November 4. A pipeline using an upcoming cyber model ran in mid-October and produced 2,308 candidate findings. Of those, 1,724 were dynamically reproduced, and variant analysis left 948 unique local privilege-escalation vulnerabilities. Local privilege escalation means an existing limited user gains greater privileges; it does not by itself give an outsider a way in. The run consumed $1,213,430 in API credits, a model-use cost that excludes researchers, infrastructure, and remediation. OpenAI says hundreds of critical reports and patches have been sent, with further submissions underway, which is not the same as 948 fixes deployed. In an early run, human reviewers found that 20 of 200 successful exploits had used a different vulnerability from the one assigned, and OpenAI added a separate check tying each test to the defect it was meant to validate. The figures are vendor-reported and have not been independently reproduced.
Narrator: The Slashdot chair and the GitHub chair agree on this one, for once, because the method is the story. A reproduced flaw tied to a specific defect is evidence; a headline count is marketing. The kernel is also the ground under yesterday's paper, where the customers are machines now and agents generate most of the tokens. Machines that write and run code at that scale will find holes at that scale, in both directions. The practical question for anyone running Linux is unglamorous: which of these patches have reached your machines, and who checked that the fix matches the finding.
Narrator: Anthropic: Claude helped solve the first prokaryotic vault structure
Narrator: Anthropic's biology team reported on November 4 that proteins related to the major vault protein assemble into vault particles in prokaryotes, the single-celled life that includes bacteria. Vaults are large hollow barrel-shaped shells first seen in complex cells in 1986, and what they do is still unknown. The team used Claude to search about 15 billion predicted proteins and found vault relatives in 29 bacterial phyla and four archaeal phyla, in fewer than 1% of sequenced species. Of 24 candidates moved into E. coli, 15 produced soluble protein and seven formed vault-like particles; cryo-electron microscopy confirmed four. The detailed structure is the phage-derived protein L05: 96 copies, 48 per half, about 9.5 megadaltons, at a nominal 3.2 angstroms, against 78 copies in mammalian vaults. A chromatography step cut isolation from about three days to about 90 minutes, and the first structural model came 34 hours into processing, with final maps about a week later. Anthropic also reports the failures plainly: one Claude-suggested protocol set the work back two weeks, and an omitted processing step produced a flawed map that researchers caught and corrected. The work is a bioRxiv preprint, not peer reviewed, the particles have been produced only in engineered E. coli, and no one has shown they form in their native hosts.
Narrator: This is the frontier beat done right. The claim is strange, a 40-year-old cellular mystery with a new branch in bacteria and phage, and it arrives with exhibits: a counted search, a physical purification, a microscope reconstruction, and the model's errors kept in the record instead of edited out. A bacterial vault that can be made quickly is a workable system for asking what vaults are for, which is the open question and stays open here. For Chris' desk the lesson travels: an AI assistant in a lab earns its keep when a human can reconstruct every decision it touched.
Narrator: Adthena: ChatGPT ads passed 200,000 placements before the holidays
Narrator: Adthena released its Holiday AI Paid Search Report on November 4. Between June and September 2026 it tracked 204,489 ChatGPT ad placements, with the advertiser base growing nearly six times in two months. Apparel, consumer electronics, and travel drove about 95% of placements, and toys and games grew fastest. Many ads met shoppers early: 83% of beauty ads, 69% of travel ads, and 67% of toys and games ads appeared against early-stage research queries rather than purchase-ready searches. On Google's side, AI Overviews appearances grew 536% in Australia, 160% in the UK and EU, and 89% in the US year over year, and Adthena counted 175,100 paid placements inside AI answers in the US, concentrated in apparel and consumer electronics. Adthena sells search intelligence, so its forecasts, including a call that Google's AI box will carry more retail ad volume than the traditional results within 18 months, are a vendor's predictions and are labeled as such here. The placement counts are the hard part of the release.
Narrator: “A year ago, there was one place to buy search ads that mattered during the holidays. Now there are three”—Phillip Thune, CEO of Adthena, November 4, 2026
Narrator: Three paid surfaces change the client conversation this quarter. A brand that only checks its Google Ads account is now missing the auction inside ChatGPT and the paid units inside Google's own AI answers. The early-stage pattern matters more than the total: ChatGPT ads are buying discovery, the moment a shopper is still forming the shortlist, which is the same moment an AI citation forms it for free. Paid and earned now compete for the same first impression, and a test budget belongs there before Black Friday prices the lesson in.
Narrator: Victorious: AI Overviews cite a top-10 page 37% of the time
Narrator: Victorious published its Q3 2026 Search Report on November 4. AI Overviews appeared on 97.5% of the category searches the agency ran on Google, up from 72.8% in Q2 on the same tracking platform. Of the sources AI Overviews cited, 37% ranked in Google's organic top 10 for the same question. For ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Copilot the figure ranged from 13% to 18%. The panel was 34 buyer-style category questions across five industries, ecommerce and retail, financial services and insurance, healthcare, legal services, and SaaS and technology, each run about 30 times at the end of September and again at the end of June, across nine AI platforms. AI Overviews cited ranking pages at a higher rate than the other platforms on 29 to 33 of the 34 questions, and third-party sources still made up most of what AI Overviews cited. Google's results shifted between searches run minutes apart, so the agency presents the overlap as a directional comparison. Victorious is an SEO and AEO agency reporting its own panel, not a census of Google.
Narrator: “For Google's AI, your organic rankings are the single biggest input.”—Michael Transon, CEO of Victorious, November 4, 2026
Narrator: Read next to the Adthena counts, the operating picture is simple enough to act on. On Google, classic ranking still feeds the AI answer more directly than it feeds any rival's answer, so SEO and AEO stay one program there. Off Google, the citation game runs through other people's pages, reviews, listings, and coverage, which is where a client's own site stops being the main lever. The 97.5% figure is the one to carry into a client meeting: on category searches, the AI answer is no longer an experiment sitting on top of the results. It is the results, and the work is being cited inside it on facts that can be checked.
Narrator: Tencent: Hy4 adds vision and computer use, weights still pending
Narrator: Tencent launched Hy4 on November 4 as the successor to Hy4 Preview, adding image understanding, tool use, and computer interaction through command-line and graphical interfaces, with training in professional design applications and features aimed at longer agent workflows. Access runs through Tencent Cloud TokenHub and Tencent applications including WorkBuddy, CodeBuddy, and Yuanbao, plus third-party API integrations, at quoted standard rates of about $0.90 per million input tokens and $2.70 per million output tokens. Tencent says updated model weights and system cards will arrive in the coming days. Until they do, the downloadable, self-hosted version of this launch does not exist yet, and the long-horizon performance claims have no independent evaluation behind them in the reporting read for this item.
Narrator: The board's rule for launches applies without malice: demo or die, and a hosted API is a demo you can rent today. Vision plus computer use in one model is a real capability step, because an agent that can read a screen can work in software that has no API. It is also the step with the sharpest failure modes, a wrong click in a real account. Anyone testing Hy4 belongs in a bounded account with no destructive permissions, measuring completed tasks and recovery after bad actions, not reading a leaderboard. When the weights and the card land, this item gets a second look on the evidence.
Narrator: IQM: all 54 qubits on Emerald entangled at once
Narrator: IQM announced on November 3 that its Emerald superconducting system has fully entangled all 54 qubits on the chip at once and that the system is available on the cloud. The demonstration prepared a Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger state, the standard benchmark for large-scale entanglement, across the whole processor. IQM measured fidelity of 0.515 at 40 qubits without readout error mitigation and 0.516 at the full 54 qubits with mitigation applied, both just above the 0.500 threshold that separates genuine entanglement from noise. Until now, a comparable demonstration on Emerald could reliably use on the order of 30 qubits in one algorithm. IQM credits about a year of calibration work, an improved gate set, dynamical decoupling, and a state-preparation routine that picks two-qubit gates by weighing fidelity against idle time, and says the work cut error rates for operations on the processor by up to 50%. These are company-reported figures from a company announcement.
Narrator: The honest read is in the margin: 0.516 is a pass, not a rout, and a GHZ state is a benchmark, not a useful computation. What makes it news is the denominator. Using every qubit on the chip in one circuit, instead of the 10 to 20 many large chips actually exercise, is the unglamorous scaling work the field keeps promising. Yesterday Regensburg cut one qubit's error with one extra measurement; today IQM kept 54 qubits coherent enough to entangle together. Fault tolerance will be built out of results shaped exactly like these two.
