Narrator: The book we are loading
Narrator: The translation is the Berean Standard Bible, the BSB: a modern, readable English Bible whose translators gave it away, dedicating the text to the public domain in April 2023. The form of it you downloaded is the one built for this exact device: the xteink3-bible set, the whole BSB as 66 individual EPUB files, one per book, numbered 01 to 66, Genesis to Revelation. It is a reader's Bible in the true sense: the verses run as flowing paragraphs instead of one verse per line, so a 3.7-inch screen wastes nothing on white space; chapter numbers sit small and bold inside the text; and each chapter is its own internal file, so the table of contents can jump straight to a chapter. The set was formatted on purpose for very small e-ink screens like this one, and the whole set is CC0, public domain, same as the Bible it carries.
Narrator: One desk confession, for the record: on November 5 I staged a different build for you, the Lantern Bible, Reader's edition, the same public-domain BSB text as one single 2.7-megabyte file. It stays in your pocket-bible folder as the spare. But 66 small books suit this reader better than one big file, and your set was made for the X3 by name, so it is the load this page teaches. Two more honest notes before hardware. Narrator: Step one: card out of the X3, into the adapter, into the X220
Narrator: With the X3 asleep or powered off, pop its microSD card out and slide the card into the USB-A adapter. Adapter into any USB-A port on the X220; the left side is where yours lives in the photograph. Mint notices removable media: the card mounts automatically and Nemo, the file manager, opens a window onto the card. That window is the workbench; everything else happens in it or in one small utility.

Narrator: Step two: check the card's filesystem before you touch anything
Narrator: Open the Disks utility from the Mint menu (its full name is GNOME Disks) and select the card in the left-hand list. Under its partition, Disks names the filesystem type. If it says FAT32, do nothing; you are already where you need to be. If it says exFAT, also do nothing: the X3 reads exFAT as well, and a working card is never improved by a format. The card that shipped in the device is 16 gigabytes and almost certainly already FAT32.
Narrator: The only case that calls for a format is a card that reads NTFS or ext4, neither of which this reader can use. Then, in Disks, click the gear icon under the partition, choose Format Partition, pick the FAT type (the one described as compatible with all systems and devices), and format. Hear this plainly: formatting erases everything on the card, permanently. Today that costs nothing, because the card is new and holds nothing of yours yet. Narrator: Step three: copy the books onto the card
Narrator: Open a second Nemo window and walk the exact path: Documents, then XTEINK, then the folder named Berean Standard Bible. Everything in it is a finished book, 66 EPUB files, numbered and ready to read. (In the raw download, that same set arrives as the folder xteink3-bible-main, and the books are its epub_books folder; your staging is tidier.) Select them all (Ctrl+A) and copy them straight onto the top level of the card. No new folder, no rearranging: the project's own loading instructions say to copy the .epub files onto the card, and the stock library will shelve them in their numbered order. Wait until the copy truly finishes and the progress bar is gone, then give it one more breath for good manners.
Narrator: Now the part that saves you an afternoon: the other things in the download folder are not for you today. build_bsb_epubs.py is the script that generated these books from the source text, and requirements.txt is its grocery list. You do not need to run the script or install anything from the list, because the books it would build are already built. The script is the printing press; you already hold the printed books. Leave the press untouched, for the hypothetical day you want to rebuild the set yourself.
Narrator: Step four: eject like you mean it
Narrator: In Nemo, click the eject arrow beside the card's name in the sidebar, or right-click the card and choose Eject. Wait until Mint tells you the card is safe to remove. Then, and only then, pull the adapter out of the X220 and take the card out of the adapter. This is the step everyone skips and the step that matters most: an EPUB yanked mid-write is a corrupted book. Ten seconds of patience is the entire price of a clean load.
Narrator: Step five: card in the X3, power on, open Genesis
Narrator: Slide the card back into the X3 and power it on. Open the library and there is the shelf: the 66 books in their numbered order, Genesis at the top, Revelation at the bottom, one tap each. Open Genesis and read a page to prove the load; the little reader takes a moment to index the new card the first time, and after that it remembers. If the shelf ever looks stale after you add books later, the community remedy is built in: open any book, choose OK, then Index, and let the reader rebuild its index.
