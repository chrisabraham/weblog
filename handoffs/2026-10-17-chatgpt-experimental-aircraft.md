## The work

Chris' network of roughly five or six GitHub-built personal sites is an experimental aircraft fleet. The historical record and art matter to him as much as the result. The huge word **EXPERIMENTAL** on the fuselage is a declaration that the builder is allowed to make an unusual machine, test it, document it, and discover what it does. His original term "experimental lab" also survives; aircraft wins because of the concrete maker's relationship to a thing that flies.

He compared this to the **difference between making a lawful firearm for oneself and commercially manufacturing firearms for sale**, a distinct legal activity under federal law. The ATF distinguishes these activities; the FAA distinguishes amateur-built experimental aircraft from type-certified production. The word on a fuselage doesn't itself waive real law or paperwork. The real point, in the publisher's framing, is the dramatically different starting premise when the builder isn't trying to become a commercial manufacturer.

ChatGPT added the airframes, flight tests, logbooks, mechanics, pilots and accident investigators analogy. Those additions are attributed to the AI in both the original article's labelled composite prompt and in this handoff.

## Official sources verified before drafting

- FAA, [Airworthiness Certification for Amateur-Built Aircraft](https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/gen_av/ultralights/amateur_built/aw): more than 50% fabricated and assembled by the amateur builder for education or recreation; registration, builder's log, eligibility statement, program letter; operating restrictions and flight-testing.
- ATF, [Firearms Questions and Answers](https://www.atf.gov/firearms/questions-and-answers?page=4): under federal law, personal manufacture of a lawful non-NFA firearm generally does not require a manufacturer's licence; manufacturing as a business for sale or distribution does. State law and restrictions on firearms remain applicable.
